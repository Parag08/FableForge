Half Gold

The golden mongoose of the Mahabharata, retold for a Mumbai monsoon.

The original

In the Ashvamedhika Parva, after Yudhishthira's great horse sacrifice, a mongoose with half its body turned to gold arrives and says the whole ceremony is not worth a little barley flour.

Years before, in a famine at Kurukshetra, a poor gleaner, his wife, his son and his son's wife gave their last meal to a hungry guest, who turned out to be Dharma. The mongoose rolled in the spilled flour and half of it turned to gold. It has visited every great sacrifice since, hoping to turn the other half.

This retelling

The sacrifice becomes a televised charity gala. The famine becomes a week of monsoon flooding that stops a delivery rider's wages. The guest knocks wearing a rain-soaked helmet.

The ending is new. The mongoose finds one more gift where nobody is counting. The Mahabharata is public domain; the words and pictures here are original.
